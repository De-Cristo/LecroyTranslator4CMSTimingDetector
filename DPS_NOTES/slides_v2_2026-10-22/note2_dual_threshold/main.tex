\TitleSlide

\begin{frame}{Abstract}
\fontsize{10}{12}\selectfont
Precision timing at the HL-LHC requires rapid, repeatable calibration as detector
response evolves. With many interactions per bunch crossing, an external
particle-arrival time reference is not readily available. The CMS barrel timing
layer (BTL) therefore motivates calibration procedures based on internal
measurements and a consistent parametrization across about 330\,000 channels.

We extend an earlier dual-threshold bench study to BTL sensors and TOFHIR2C
readout exposed to a 180\,GeV pion beam. Two threshold-crossing times provide a
leading-edge slope proxy. Its mean dependence on charge defines an inverse-slope
walk correction with one amplitude parameter, AWC, per channel and configuration.

Comparison with independent microchannel-plate (MCP) reference calibrations shows
good agreement between the correction shapes. Internal charge and slope
information provide candidate predictors of AWC, with the MCP results serving
as benchmarks. These results support development of a reference-free calibration
procedure; predictive precision and transfer to other operating conditions
require further validation.
\Source{BTL and TOFHIR2C: Refs.~\cite{MTDTDR,TOFHIR2}; dual-threshold motivation and bench study: Ref.~\cite{DualThreshold}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Charge-based calibration works, but its coefficients vary}
\normalsize
The MCP reference note obtains good walk descriptions with a quadratic in
inverse charge~\cite{ReferenceNote}:
\[

\]

\begin{frame}{TOFHIR2C measures the leading-edge slope with two thresholds}
\normalsize
Larger pulses cross a fixed threshold earlier: the timestamp depends on amplitude~\cite{Leo}.
Two-threshold timing has earlier implementations~\cite{Grund,Frolov};
TOFHIR2C provides both crossings~\cite{TOFHIR2,DualThreshold}.
\[
I_1<I_2,\qquad t_1<t_2,\qquad
S=\frac{I_2-I_1}{t_2-t_1}\quad[\mu\mathrm{A/ns}].
\]
Locally, an approximately linear leading edge gives
\[
\Delta t\simeq\frac{\Delta I}{S}.
\]
\textbf{Physical model:} describe amplitude walk with an inverse slope,
rather than an independent polynomial shape for every channel.
\Source{$S$ is a finite-interval slope proxy, not the instantaneous derivative.
The dual-threshold sample uses $\Delta I=7.8125\,\mu$A from the implemented threshold conversion.
The fitted AWC need not equal this threshold separation.
\Code{Q_Walk/qwalk_core.py}{GitHub: timing and threshold reconstruction}~\cite{github_repo}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One amplitude describes walk once the slope is calibrated}
\large
\[
W(Q)=\frac{\mathrm{AWC}}{\overline S(Q)},\qquad
t_{\rm corr}=t_{\rm measured}-W(Q).
\]
\normalsize
\begin{itemize}
\item Internal hits determine $\overline S(Q)$, the mean slope at a given charge, for each SiPM.
\item One parameter, AWC (in $\mu$A), sets the walk magnitude.
\item AWC is constant per SiPM and configuration; the correction varies with $Q$.
\item Channel timing offsets are a separate calibration.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
\textbf{The remaining task is to predict AWC from internal observables.}
Use the MCP-calibrated channels to develop and test that prediction.
\Source{Apply the model within its calibrated charge range, with $\overline S(Q)>0$.
The slope fit alone does not fix AWC. The common functional form follows the dual-threshold model in Ref.~\cite{DualThreshold}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]{Candidate 2: a physics-motivated AWC predictor}
\small
\textbf{Physics motivation:} similar scintillation pulses and front-end shaping motivate
testing a shared timing sensitivity to the same fractional charge change.
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{.43\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=28mm,keepaspectratio]{figures/internal_predictor.pdf}
\fontsize{9}{10.5}\selectfont
\[
K\simeq-\frac{dW}{d\ln Q}\bigg|_{Q_*},\quad
\mathrm{AWC}_{\rm pred}=\frac{K\overline S(Q_*)^2}{c_1Q_*}.
\]
\end{column}
\begin{column}{.55\textwidth}
\fontsize{9.5}{11}\selectfont
\textbf{Required assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item $W=\mathrm{AWC}/\overline S$ holds locally, with constant AWC per SiPM.
\item The fitted $\overline S(Q)=c_0+c_1Q$ is reliable near $Q_*$.
\item $Q_*$, the selected-charge median per SiPM, samples comparable pulse regions.
\item Similar pulses and operating conditions allow a common $K$ across SiPMs and bars.
\end{enumerate}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\Source{Ten SiPMs; hold out each of six bars and learn $K$ from the others' MCP-calibrated AWC.
RMS error: \PredictorRMSE\,$\mu$A; fractional RMS: \PredictorFraction\%.
Dashed line: equality. Horizontal bars: conditional reference-AWC intervals, not prediction intervals.
Not a universal law: full uncertainties, correlations and transfer to new configurations remain unvalidated.}
\end{frame}
